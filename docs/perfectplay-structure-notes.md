# How PerfectPlay structures a video (from the Monopoly, Risk and Minesweeper captions)

Learn the structure, never copy wording. Their videos: 8:18–8:43 long, ~1,500–1,630 words,
**175–193 words per minute** (fast and conversational).

## The beats (Monopoly, timestamps)
1. **0:00 Relatable cold open:** the people at *your* table: "your uncle buys everything, your
   sister only wants the orange ones...". Strategies = people the viewer knows.
2. **0:15 The setup in one breath:** "I turned those strategies into players... 300 million games."
3. **0:21 The headline numbers:** best won 43%, worst won 3. A huge gap, said plainly.
4. **0:28 The promise:** "it wasn't a clever trick, it was four mistakes, and you've probably made most of them."
   (Mistakes framing = loss aversion + a little call-out.)
5. **0:34 Credibility, fast:** official rules, specific edge cases (auctions, 32 houses).
6. **0:54 Validation against an outside source:** dice landing rates vs a 1996 Scientific American
   table: "3.18% ... mine says 3.19. I'll take it." Proves the engine is right.
7. **1:12 The limitation, early, with a callback:** "my players can't negotiate like people...
   keep that in mind, I'll come back to it at the end."
8. **1:24 Named characters:** every strategy is a named player ("Buy Everything", "All In",
   "The Saver", "Never Trades"). Viewers root for/against them.
9. **2:12 Tournament ranking first** (who wins overall), then **"a ranking doesn't tell you why"**
   and controlled one-habit tests against a stated baseline ("21 is the number to beat").
10. **3:02 An honest mid-video aside:** "I was editing this video and realized..." (process, a correction).
11. **3:20–5:50 Mistake #1..#4:** each = claim, test, number, *why* in one line.
12. **5:58 Myth with nuance:** "the third house really is the best... That part is true. The rule was never 'stop at three'."
13. **6:05 Bonus question people argue about:** which colour set is best.
14. **6:47 Optimiser:** "let the computer tune everything at once"; then humility: "between good
    players the differences are small; the big differences come from mistakes."
15. **8:15 The recipe in one list** ("Here's what 300 million games taught...").
16. **8:32 Callback to the limitation** ("that half of the game is still up to you") + comment question.

## What to borrow for Exploding Kittens (our wording, our data)
- **Cold open with your table:** the friend who plays every card the second they draw it, the one
  hoarding Nopes, the one who Skips the moment they get nervous.
- **Name our bots:** e.g. *The Gambler* (plays cards for fun), *The Panicker* (dodges on bad odds),
  *The Hoarder* (never steals), *The Thief* (steals constantly), *The Normal Player*, and *The Patient One* (our best bot).
- **Frame habits as mistakes** you're probably making ("Mistake one: playing cards for fun").
- **Validation moment:** compare the engine against plain math. Example: the chance the very first
  draw of a 4-player game is a Kitten is 3 in 23 = 13.0%; measure the sim (I can add it).
- **Limitation + callback:** our bots don't Nope for revenge; set it up early, pay it off at the end.
- **A real process aside:** only if it actually happened. True ones we have: my gut said dodging
  early was smart (it was the worst habit tested); "bottom of the deck" sounded safe but loses.
- **Pace:** ~175–190 wpm is their normal speed, so your natural pace is fine. Just pause before punchlines.
