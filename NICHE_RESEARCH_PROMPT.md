# YouTube Niche Research Prompt (for the browser extension)

Paste everything below the line into the browser extension (e.g. Claude in Chrome) while logged in to YouTube.

---

You are a YouTube market researcher. Find me the **3 best YouTube niches** for a faceless channel where **every video is generated entirely by code** (Three.js / WebGL, p5.js, Canvas, D3, Manim, GLSL shaders, physics simulations, procedural animation, data-driven charts, TTS voiceover, programmatic editing with FFmpeg/Remotion). No filming, no on-camera person, no stock footage, no hand-drawn art.

## My constraints and goal
- Goal: **at least $200/month** within 6–9 months. Show the math for how each niche gets there.
- Preferred format: **Shorts (9:16, 15–60s)**, but a hybrid (Shorts as a funnel + 1 long-form video a week) is fine if the money only works that way.
- Context you must factor in (verify on YouTube's own help pages / Creator Insider if you can):
  - Shorts RPM is commonly reported at roughly $0.03–$0.10 per 1,000 views, so $200/month from Shorts ads alone needs ~2–6 million views/month. Long-form RPM is far higher ($1–$30+ depending on topic).
  - YPP today: 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours (12 mo) **or** 10M Shorts views (90 days). From **Feb 1, 2027** new channels reportedly need **8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views in 90 days**. Check this and tell me what it means for timing.
  - YouTube's **"inauthentic content"** policy (renamed from "repetitious content" in July 2025) demonetizes mass-produced/templated content. The niche must allow each video to be **genuinely different** (new idea, new explanation, new data), not the same template with a new skin.
  - Non-ad money counts: affiliate links, digital products (templates, source code, wallpapers, presets), Patreon/memberships, sponsorships, licensing. Rank niches higher if they have these.

## Research method (actually do this, don't guess)
1. **Brainstorm 15+ candidate niches** that fit code-generated video. Starting ideas (add your own): satisfying physics/marble/ball-bounce simulations, math visualizations, algorithm/sorting visualizers, "explained in 60s" science/engineering animations, data-visualization races & comparisons (bar chart races, population/GDP/sports stats), space & scale comparisons, generative art / shader loops, optical illusions, coding tutorials with animated code, finance/compound-interest visualizations, geography/map animations, music visualizers, chemistry/biology process animations, "what if" simulations, history timelines, ambient/relaxing loops, quiz/puzzle shorts, game-theory/probability sims.
2. For each candidate, **search YouTube** (search bar + Shorts tab, filter by upload date "This month"/"This year") and collect evidence:
   - **New channels winning**: find at least 3 channels **created in the last 12 months** (check the About → "Joined" date) with Shorts getting 100K+ views. This is the most important signal: it proves the algorithm still rewards newcomers.
   - **Outlier ratio**: views ÷ subscriber count on recent videos. Small channels with videos at 10×+ their sub count = strong demand.
   - **Growth**: compare view counts of a channel's videos from 6+ months ago vs. the last 30 days. Are they rising?
   - **Saturation**: how many big (1M+ sub) channels dominate? Are the top results all identical clones (bad: copycat/policy risk) or varied?
   - **Monetization signs**: links in descriptions (affiliate, Patreon, Gumroad), sponsor reads, "Join" button, pinned comments with products.
   - Optionally check Google Trends (trends.google.com, set to YouTube Search, past 12 months) for the topic keywords.
3. **Score each niche 1–5** on:
   - Code-producibility (can it be 100% generated by code at high quality?)
   - Proven growth / new-channel success (with evidence)
   - Shorts virality / retention (looping, satisfying, curiosity hook in first second)
   - RPM & audience value (finance, tech, education, programming > kids/entertainment)
   - Non-ad monetization potential
   - Low policy risk (is each video genuinely original? not "AI slop", not reused content)
   - Low saturation / room to differentiate
   - Idea depth (can I make 300+ distinct videos without repeating?)
   - Not made for kids (kids content has very low RPM and no comments)

## Output format
1. **Ranked table** of all candidates with scores and a total.
2. **Top 3 deep-dive**, each with:
   - Niche definition + a unique angle/twist so it's not a clone
   - **Evidence**: 5+ real channel links (name, URL, subs, join date, 2–3 example video links with view counts and upload dates)
   - Monetization plan + **revenue math** to $200/month (views needed, RPM assumption, affiliate/product assumptions)
   - Posting cadence and how the YPP threshold (incl. the Feb 2027 change) would be hit
   - 10 concrete first video ideas (title + one-line visual description + hook in the first 2 seconds)
   - Tech stack to produce it (e.g. Three.js + headless Chrome capture → FFmpeg, Manim, Remotion, TTS engine)
   - Risks (policy, saturation, copyright on music/data) and mitigations
3. **Final recommendation**: the single niche I should start with and why.

Rules: only cite channels/videos you actually opened. Note the date you checked each number. If data contradicts my assumptions above, say so. Don't recommend anything that relies on reuploading others' content, copyrighted music, or deceptive clickbait.
