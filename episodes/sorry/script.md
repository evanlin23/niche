## Cold open
[o1] Everybody plays Sorry! a little differently.
[o2] Your little brother moves whichever pawn is furthest behind. Your aunt swaps with the leader every time she draws an eleven. And somebody always saves the Sorry! card for whoever's winning.
[o3] So I turned those habits into bots, gave them the official rules, and had them play over a billion games.
[o4] Here's the headline. At a table of four, where a fair share is one game in four, the best bot won a third of its games. The worst won one in ten.
[o5] And Sorry! is supposed to be all luck. It isn't. It comes down to a few mistakes, and you're probably making one of them.

## The game in ten seconds
[g1] Quick rules. Draw a card, move a pawn. Get all four of your pawns from Start to Home. Land on someone and they go back to Start. Sorry.

## Can you trust it
[c1] My version follows Hasbro's classic rules. Forty five cards. No sixes, no nines. Only a one or a two gets you out of Start, and a two lets you draw again.
[c2] Slides, the seven you can split, the eleven that swaps, and the four that moves you backwards. All in there.
[c3] To check the engine I used plain math. With four players, the chance that nobody gets a pawn out in the whole first round is the chance that the first four cards are all useless. On paper that's thirty nine point five percent. Over hundreds of millions of first rounds, my engine says thirty nine point five.
[c4] One thing my bots can't do is trash talk, or gang up on the leader on purpose. Keep that in mind. I'll come back to it at the end.

## Meet the players
[p1] Here's the table.
[p2] The Normal player always makes the move that gets them furthest, and bumps people whenever it can.
[p3] The Spreader moves the pawn that's furthest behind. The Runner pushes its front pawn home first.
[p4] The Switcher swaps with the leader on every eleven. The Avenger always uses Sorry! on the leader's best pawn.
[p5] And the Careful player won't stop right in front of an opponent if it can help it.
